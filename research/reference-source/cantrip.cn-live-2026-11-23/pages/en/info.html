About Cantrip

Why Cantrip

For the past six months, my job title has been “AI product manager.” Product manager in name; in practice, a do-everything engineer. Frontend, backend, product: if it needed doing, I did it.

Whenever I built something, I wanted it to look a bit nicer. But I couldn't say what “nice” actually was, so all I could do was tell the AI, again and again:

“Go look at how Apple designs stuff, come on. This is way too ugly.”

The AI tried really hard. Then it came back ugly in a whole new way.

So I started learning UI, bit by bit, and looking at designs that are actually good. And I noticed that each of them has a name.

Progressive blur. Spring bounce. Frosted glass.

Once I started saying those names to the AI, everything changed.

Say the right word, and it works like a spell.

The first time I used Claude Design, the page built itself right in front of me, block by block, shifting, bouncing.

I was so excited I shouted: Holy crap Σ(°ロ°)

Right then I knew what this site had to be called.

Cantrip.

In D&D, it's the smallest spell there is: no spell slot, cast at will, as often as you like. A bit like silent casting 🧙‍♀️

Why not just call it Magic? Magic is too ordinary (and the domain was taken long ago).

Besides, a good interface was never a grand, earth-shaking spell. It's the bounce of a switch, a blur in just the right amount.

It's the little cantrips that make you smile without meaning to.

Here I want to share what I think about UI while building products, the good designs I come across, and anything else that's beautiful. If you've ever told an AI “this is way too ugly,” I hope you find your spell here.

What gets in

Looking good isn't enough to get in. A piece goes up only if I can say what it saves the user and why it feels cared for. When the author has published the source, the detail page links to it; when they haven't, you get the video they posted.

Originals and credit

Of the 99 pieces here, 34 are rebuilt from work a designer published, credited “Original by” on the detail page with a link back to the source; 10 draw on a product or a published article, credited “Based on”; 50 are the authors' own videos or images, shown as posted, credited “By” with a link to the original post; the other 5 are my own designs. I wrote the code for every demo myself.

If a credit is wrong, or an original author wants something taken down, write to hi@cantrip.cn and I'll sort it out within 24 hours.
